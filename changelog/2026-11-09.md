# 2026-11-09

- [2026-11-09] [Fix] **A Valkey that stops answering no longer stops every signed-in request.** When Valkey's container is paused, or a network drops its packets, the connection stays open and nothing answers on it, and nothing limited how long a Valkey command could wait. Every signed-in tRPC and REST call checks its principal through the cache first, so each one waited for good, and so did password sign-in, MFA and MCP client registration on their attempt limit: with `docker pause`, a signed-in call made no progress in 8 minutes. The cache and the attempt limiter now give a command 2 seconds. After that a cache read counts as a miss, so the principal comes from Postgres, and the limiter refuses, as it does when Valkey is down. Only calls made in those first 2 seconds wait, because the cache then closes the silent connection and skips Valkey until a new one answers: with Valkey paused, 296 signed-in calls over 30 seconds answered in 2 ms at the median, and the cache was back within milliseconds of Valkey. A cache that started while Valkey couldn't be reached now starts working when Valkey does, where it stayed off until the app restarted. Sign-in also refuses within 2 seconds when Valkey is stopped, where it could take 10. A command still unanswered when its connection closes now fails rather than being sent again on the next one, since its caller may already have been told it failed. **While Valkey isn't answering, a change that ends sessions can't remove the account's cached principal**: the change applies at once to every request, since they read Postgres then, but a copy Valkey still holds when it answers again is used for the rest of its 15 seconds. `tests/storage/valkey-not-answering.test.ts` covers this against real Postgres and Valkey.
- [2026-11-09] [Fix] **A Valkey that stops answering no longer holds socket connections, an admin's changes to a user, or a job's end.** The socket security store and the execution-token revocation store have ioredis clients of their own, and neither had a time limit. With Valkey paused or cut off, every Socket.IO handshake waited on its ticket. Every change that ends sessions or terminals (a user's password, role or status, signing a user out, deleting a user, a role's permissions, a server's address or credentials, deleting a server) waited on the store's check before writing, and each went on to commit when Valkey answered again, long after its request had been given up on. Every job's end waited on its revocation marker after it had committed, so `jobs.cancel` and the orchestrator's job reports didn't answer, and the worker held each in-process job's slot and started nothing after ten. Both clients now give a command 2 seconds, as the cache does since the previous fix. A handshake is refused, and an admin's change is refused before anything is written, as when Valkey is stopped. A revocation that can't be published after its change committed is logged rather than turned into an error, because the change is in effect and each socket server disconnects that user at its next check of connected users, every 10 seconds. A job's marker that can't be written is written once Valkey answers. Measured through the real routes with Valkey paused, each of these answered or refused in 2.0 seconds where none had answered in 10, and none committed late. With Valkey paused before their first connection, they take 2 seconds instead of 5. The socket server's revocation subscriber now subscribes again on each new connection itself: under the same limits, ioredis would have left it subscribed to nothing after a resubscribe went unanswered, and the timed-out resubscribe would have ended the process. `tests/storage/valkey-security-stores-not-answering.test.ts` covers this against real Postgres and Valkey.
